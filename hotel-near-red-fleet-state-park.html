What to Do at Red Fleet State Park

Red Fleet Reservoir sits at 5,600 feet in a landscape of Navajo sandstone that glows deep red at sunrise and sunset. The park is famous for its dinosaur trackway — over 200 dilophosaurus tracks preserved in sandstone along the northeast shore, dating back approximately 200 million years. The reservoir is also one of the warmest swimming spots in the Uintah Basin, making it a local favorite from late May through September.

  • Dinosaur Trackway — 200+ preserved dilophosaurus footprints
  • Swimming and cliff jumping at red sandstone beaches
  • Kayaking and stand-up paddleboarding
  • Fishing — rainbow trout, bass, and bluegill
  • Hiking trails along the reservoir rim
  • Camping — tent and RV sites with lake views

Pro Tip: The dinosaur trackway trail is about a 1.5-mile round trip from the parking area. Go in the morning when the light hits the tracks at an angle — they're much easier to see. Then head back to the hotel (15 minutes) for breakfast.
